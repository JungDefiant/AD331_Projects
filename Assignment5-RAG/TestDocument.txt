In the basement of a humble Midwestern home, a group of young men gather around a table. They are enraptured by the speaker at the head of the table, describing the hallowed halls of a fictional dungeon. The speaker was Dave Arneson, a history student who was a passionate about miniatures wargaming. The men around the table were the players of the first tabletop roleplaying game, including his friend Gary Gygax.
	Arneson would begin running campaigns in 1970 that modified the rules of an existing wargame called Braunstein. Him and his friends would use this ruleset to make up stories by pretending to be characters in a medieval fantasy world. Unlike their modern forms, players would be split between taking the roles of adventurers and villains, but many of the principles remained the same. Players would describe what their character is doing and a referee would describe what happens, occasionally asking for a roll of a die if something has a chance of failure.
